# 02 — Secondary marketing, auth and onboarding audit

Scope: /pricing, /about, /docs, /contact, /legal/{security,dpa,subprocessors}, /privacy, /terms, /thank-you, 404, loading, site footer, cookie banner, /login, /signup (+ verify), /onboarding.
Method: I read the source and fetched the rendered HTML from `localhost:3000` (master), then extracted the visible copy. I applied the criteria from frontend-design, better-ui, emil-design-eng and ui-ux-pro-max. I had no screenshots, so I did not verify the visual rendering at breakpoints or in light mode. Those findings come from reading the code.

## 1. Verdict

**Score: 5.5 / 10.** The visuals are mostly credible: a Linear-style dark UI, a consistent lime accent, correct per-credit math, a real split-screen auth layout, and an onboarding flow that shows scored accounts before you enter the app. The problem is what the pages say. They tell two stories that contradict each other:

- **/about** says "$0 outside funding raised", "one room", "v0.1" and "Person on payroll (in a manner of speaking)".
- **/docs, /contact and /legal/dpa** claim a SOC 2 Type II report, a Vanta trust portal, sales engineers, HubSpot and Salesforce, 99.97% uptime, bounties paid within 14 days, and an office HQ.
- **/legal/security** says SOC 2 and the bug bounty are "Not scheduled".

An investor who clicks through three pages will notice. On top of that, the docs describe endpoints that don't exist.

## 2. What's already good (keep)

- **Token discipline.** The marketing pages use `var(--brand)` and `#DFFF00` consistently, the hover is `#E8FF40`, and the off-brand `#D4FF3D` never appears.
- **Pricing math is correct and shown per credit.** $29/500 = $0.058, $79/2,500 = $0.032, $199/8,000 = $0.025. The FAQ explains credits, what happens on a 402, cancellation and expiry in plain language.
- **Auth layout.** `app/(auth)/layout.tsx` puts a value panel on the left ("Know why an account is ready before you reach out.") and the form on the right. It has labelled fields, a password show/hide toggle with an aria-label, correct `autocomplete` values, `one-time-code` on the verification input, and busy states ("Creating account…" / "Verifying…").
- **Time to value in onboarding.** Four steps take the user to scored accounts *before* they enter the app ("See scored accounts — Before you enter the app. That's the deal."). The wizard also promises resume ("Every answer is saved…"). This is the strongest product moment in this section. Keep it and make it the centre of the demo.
- **404 and thank-you pages** use the product's vocabulary ("This page scored a 0.") and each gives two clear next actions. Short and on-brand.
- **Cookie banner** gives Reject and Accept equal weight, never flashes into server-rendered HTML, and can be reopened from the footer.
- **Legal pages** have a table of contents, a version, an effective date and cross-links. Subprocessors is a real table. The *idea* of an honest security page is good.

## 4. Top 3 highest-leverage changes (next few days)

1. **One honest story across the site (M1–M5, M24). About 1 day.** Go page by page and delete every claim that isn't true: SOC 2 report, Vanta, PGP, bounty, sales engineers, HubSpot/Salesforce, uptime and latency stats, webhooks, SDK, the `iiq` prefixes. Rewrite /about from a "bedroom diary" into a founder-led mission page. The investor is judging trust as much as UI, and right now three pages contradict each other. This is copy work, not design, and it has the biggest impact.
2. **Unified marketing chrome and pricing (M7–M12, M20). About half a day to a day.** Use one `LandingNav` everywhere and fix the dead links, the footer and the missing `#customers` anchor. Build /pricing and the landing pricing from `lib/billing-plans.ts`, with number formatting, an Agency column, top-up packs and a compact comparison table. Consistency between pages is what makes a site "feel funded".
3. **Polish the first product moment (M13–M18). About half a day.** Add a Google mark, a Terms line, "sent to name@co.com", a 6-box OTP with resend cooldown, a smaller mascot, "Step X of 4", the real logo in onboarding and a narrow-width layout. Then rehearse the demo path: sign up, verify, onboarding, scored accounts, dashboard. In the demo, never open `/onboarding` signed out (it shows `preview@example.com` and "Layout preview only"), and dismiss the cookie banner before the call.

## 5. Design references

- **Pricing:** Linear pricing (4 columns, one highlighted plan, a full comparison table below), Clay pricing (credits-based: plan cards plus a credit explainer plus top-ups), Apollo pricing (credit-based sales intel, annual toggle).
- **Docs:** Resend docs and Stripe API reference (three-pane layout with real, copy-paste examples that work), Clearbit/Breeze API docs for enrichment response objects.
- **Trust/security:** Vercel's security page and Cal.com's /security (short controls lists, no internal admissions). Early-stage companies such as Attio and Plain present "roadmap" items as outcomes, not gaps.
- **Auth:** Linear and Vercel signup (form first, one line of value, OAuth with brand marks); Clerk's own OTP pattern (6-box, shows the email, resend timer).
- **Onboarding:** Attio and Common Room onboarding (ICP first, then value immediately); Clay's "see your first enriched rows" moment is the same idea as VesperWise's results step.
- **About:** Linear "Method" / Raycast "About" (mission, principles, small team photo, no vanity stats).
